## 01 — Professional profile

I am **Salvatore Sinatra**, a freelance photographer and visual artist based in **Caltagirone, Sicily, Italy**. My independent practice connects photography, visual arts, editorial communication, digital imaging and creative technologies.

My work spans fine art, editorial, portrait, black-and-white, urban, architectural and landscape photography. Rather than treating an image as a purely descriptive record, I approach it as a deliberate construction of light, space, timing, form, atmosphere and presence.

My GitHub profile brings together personal websites, visual-identity projects, independent music archives and experimental publishing workflows. These repositories represent an evolving creative practice, not a claim of formal software-engineering credentials.

## 02 — Creative identity and artistic statement

My photographic language prioritizes visual coherence, intentional composition and the expressive relationship between the subject and its surroundings. Black and white is a central element of my work, but it is not simply an aesthetic filter: tonal separation, contrast, texture and negative space are part of how an image communicates.

**Core principles**

- **Presence over perfection:** seek images with perceptual and emotional weight rather than purely technical polish.
- **Authorship:** make intentional choices at every stage, from framing to final presentation.
- **Integrity:** preserve the identity and provenance of original photographs.
- **Editorial precision:** combine visual hierarchy, typography and whitespace into consistent presentation systems.
- **Research and verification:** distinguish documented information from interpretation, hypothesis and material awaiting confirmation.
- **Independent publishing:** build coherent, accessible archives with transparent attribution.

### Fine art and monochrome

I use monochrome photography to explore structure and perception through luminance, texture and contrast. My approach values subtle tonal transitions as much as dramatic shadow, depending on the subject and visual intention.

### Portrait and editorial

Portraiture is a dialogue between identity, environment and the photographer's decisions. Editorial projects require visual consistency across selection, sequencing, captions and publication.

### Urban, architectural and landscape work

In urban and architectural settings, I observe geometric relationships, human scale, repetitions and interruptions. Landscape photography extends that attention to light, depth, atmosphere and spatial memory.

### Digital image integrity

I distinguish documentary or original photographic work from AI-generated visual material. When faithful reproduction is required, I avoid unrequested cropping, recoloring, synthetic reconstruction or other transformations that would change the author's original image.

### Editorial architecture

A mature archive can include a homepage, artist biography, chronological discography, individual album and single pages, format and edition variants, image galleries, official-source references, streaming links, a bibliography, a changelog and a rights notice.

### Image cataloguing

An image inventory should distinguish a candidate URL from an actually retrieved and verified file. Relevant metadata includes:

| Field | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Asset identifier | Stable internal reference |
| Artist / project | Subject of the asset |
| Source URL | Original source page or direct asset location |
| Attribution | Photographer, designer, publisher or rights holder where known |
| Dimensions | Verified width and height, not estimated |
| File format | JPEG, PNG, WebP, TIFF or other |
| Copyright / license | Verified rights information; unknown where unavailable |
| Duplicate status | Hash or perceptual-similarity review |
| Verification status | Verified, pending review or excluded |
| Editorial notes | Context, release, date, edition and caveats |

A public URL is not evidence that an image is freely licensed or suitable for redistribution.

## 10 — Photography equipment and workflow

My photographic equipment includes the **Canon EOS R50** and **Canon EOS 2000D / Rebel T7**, alongside the **Canon EF-S 18–135 mm**, **Canon EF 75–300 mm** and **Canon EF 50 mm f/1.8** lenses. Lens-to-body compatibility and the need for an adapter must be assessed for each configuration.

### Reference workflow

1. Define the photographic or editorial intention.
2. Assess subject, environment, available light and compositional constraints.
3. Capture original files with attention to exposure, focus and framing.
4. Review and select images based on authorship, expression and visual coherence.
5. Apply controlled processing while preserving the original image's identity.
6. Export for the appropriate medium, resolution and color-management requirements.
7. Archive originals, derivatives, metadata and attribution records.
8. Publish within a consistent visual and editorial framework.

## 11 — Documentation and project governance

I treat documentation as part of the project rather than as a secondary deliverable.

### Recommended conventions

- A descriptive README for every independent repository.
- Clear distinction between planned, in-progress, verified and published material.
- A source register for research-derived statements and media.
- An explicit record of copyright, permissions and licensing.
- Versioned changes and descriptive commit messages.
- Deployment instructions and known limitations.
- Accessibility and responsive-layout checks.
- Separation of original work, third-party content and AI-generated assets.

### Quality criteria

A project should be understandable to a first-time visitor, technically reproducible where possible, editorially coherent, and honest about what is complete versus still in development.
